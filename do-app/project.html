Data Deserts

Where evidence is missing, decisions become harder.

Data Deserts reveals the uneven geographic and temporal coverage of ecological, agricultural, and public-health data—and asks how transparent, responsible AI might help researchers identify gaps without disguising uncertainty as knowledge.

The central idea

From scattered observations to visible gaps

Living systems connect biodiversity, food production, water, climate and human health. The animation shows how gaps in one domain limit our understanding of the whole system.

What the project does

Data Deserts combines eight sources into a single map of country-year coverage. Radial charts make overlap—and its absence—visible across ecology, agriculture, and public health.

02

Why gaps matter

Evidence is not collected evenly. Sparse or disconnected records can hide change, weaken comparisons, and leave communities under-represented in research and policy.

03

Where AI may help

AI can detect coverage patterns, identify high-value places and years for new collection, connect compatible sources, and generate testable estimates with explicit uncertainty.

04

What AI cannot do

Predictions are not observations. Responsible use must preserve provenance, expose bias and uncertainty, protect sensitive data, and direct investment toward collecting missing evidence.
